Sheltie Blowing Coat: How Long It Lasts and a Week-by-Week De-Shedding Routine

A Sheltie blowing coat drops its soft undercoat in clumps over a few weeks, not in one afternoon. The useful questions are how long this round is likely to last, whether it has peaked yet, and how to get the loose hair out without wearing down the harsh outer coat that should stay.

Quick answer: expect the heavy stage to run for several weeks. Owners on a breed forum describe roughly a month to six weeks before the dog starts to look normal again, and one groomer names April to June as the heaviest season in the Northern Hemisphere. Brush in short daily sessions, line brush to the skin, and track how much hair comes out so you can see the peak pass.

How long does a Sheltie blowing coat last?

Several weeks is the realistic answer, and there is no verified fixed number. The figures available come from groomers, breeders and owners rather than controlled studies, so treat them as ranges to plan around.

Writing for Groomer to Groomer, Jennifer Bishop Jenkins says Shelties need extra brushing and de-shedding especially from April to June in the Northern Hemisphere, when they are blowing off the winter undercoat most heavily. That describes a season of up to three months, not three months of daily clumps. In a Sheltie Forums thread about blowing coat, one owner says a female takes a good month to start looking normal again, and another puts regrowth at about six weeks after the loss.

How often will your Sheltie blow coat?

Usually once or twice a year, with sex and hormones changing the pattern. According to Incendio Shetland Sheepdogs, a breeder, males carry a longer coat but generally blow it once a year, while females carry a shorter, denser coat and generally blow it every 6 to 9 months, right after coming into season.

That is one kennel's experience with mostly intact dogs, so your own dog may differ. Some owners on the forum thread above say a dog blew coat only once as a youngster and has simply needed brushing since. Keep the dates from your tuft log from year to year and you will have a better forecast than any breed average.

Young dogs and the puppy coat

The first big change can look like a coat blow. The same Groomer to Groomer article says coat change in Shelties may start as early as six months, as longer and stronger adult guard hairs come in along the spine. If your dog is still young, build tolerance first with a step-by-step puppy brushing plan.

What are the signs the blow is starting?

The first sign is hair that lifts out in soft tufts instead of single strands. You may find them on the hindquarters, in the ruff or on the dog's bedding before the floor tells you.

  • The comb comes out full from a spot that was easy last week.
  • Pale, woolly fluff shows through the darker outer coat.
  • Small tangles appear behind the ears, under the legs and in the trousers.
  • The coat feels bulkier for a few days, then suddenly flatter.

Normal seasonal shedding thins the coat evenly. Bare patches, sore skin or constant scratching are not part of a coat blow and are a reason to call your vet.

Splitting the dog into zones

Divide the body so no session turns into a wrestling match. A simple rotation is: day one, chest and ruff; day two, left side; day three, right side; day four, hindquarters, trousers and tail. During the peak, add a quick pass over the hindquarters to whichever zone is due.

When to stop for the day

Stop while the dog is still relaxed. If the skin looks pink, or the same patch has had many passes, leave it until tomorrow. Loose hair that stays in today will still come out; irritated skin makes tomorrow harder.

How do you line brush a Sheltie during a coat blow?

Line brushing means parting the coat in a horizontal line down to the skin and brushing a thin layer at a time. Bishop Jenkins writes that a thorough line brushing of the torso gently removes shedding undercoat, and suggests a long-tined soft slicker with pliable tines.

  1. Teach the dog to lie on one side on a non-slip surface.
  2. Start low, near the belly or a leg, and hold the hair above your working line out of the way with your free hand.
  3. Brush the thin layer below your hand from the skin outward, without scraping the skin.
  4. Let down another thin layer and repeat, moving up toward the spine.
  5. Run a steel comb through the section. If it stops, that section is not finished.
  6. Turn the dog over and repeat on the other side.

A light mist of water or grooming spray can reduce static while you work. For choosing pin length, pad firmness and comb, see our Sheltie brush and comb comparison.

Undercoat rake: useful, with limits

The AKC's guide to double-coated dogs suggests a grooming rake or quality steel comb to remove loose undercoat, and a slicker where the coat is denser. Use the rake with short, light strokes and only where loose hair is waiting. If it drags, put it down and return to line brushing. Be cautious with bladed de-shedding tools, which can cut the outer coat you want to keep.

Should you bathe a Sheltie while it is blowing coat?

A bath with a complete blow-dry can move a lot of loose undercoat at once, usually early in the peak. Groomers handle a Sheltie as a "bath-brush" dog: bathe, condition, blow-dry and brush out, with trimming mostly limited to feet and hocks.

  1. Brush out tangles before water touches them, because wet knots tighten.
  2. Wet the coat to the skin with lukewarm water, shampoo, then rinse longer than seems necessary.
  3. Towel by pressing, not rubbing.
  4. Dry on a low heat setting, with the nozzle angled so air pushes the coat back and down in its natural direction rather than straight at the skin.
  5. Line brush once the coat is fully dry.

The drying angle comes from the same groomer's advice to protect the lie of the coat along the back. If the dryer is new to your dog, introduce the sound from across the room with treats on a few earlier days.

Should you shave or clip to speed things up?

No. Grooming experts quoted by the AKC generally advise against shaving double-coated breeds, since the undercoat can outgrow the topcoat and leave an uneven coat. If a shave has already happened, our owner-reported regrowth timeline shows what the months afterwards can look like.

For matting you cannot safely brush out, ask a professional groomer rather than reaching for scissors. Mats sit close to the skin and it is easy to cut it.

How do you keep the house manageable?

Take the hair off the dog before it takes itself off. A few habits make the weeks easier.

  • Brush on a sheet or outdoors and bag the hair as you go.
  • Wash the dog's bedding more often during the peak.
  • Vacuum the resting spots first, where most tufts collect.
  • Brush after a walk, when the dog is calmer. Our daily Sheltie exercise checker helps you plan that timing.

What mistakes make a coat blow drag on?

Brushing only the surface

A glossy top layer can hide a packed undercoat. The comb check is the only way to know you reached the skin.

Saving everything for the weekend

Six quiet days give loose hair time to knot behind the ears and in the trousers. Fifteen or twenty minutes a day is usually easier than one long session.

Pulling against a mat

Hold the hair at the base, work the tips first and stop if the dog flinches repeatedly.

Judging progress by the floor

The floor depends on when you last vacuumed. The amount in the brush, scored the same way each time, is a better signal.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a Sheltie blow coat?

Plan on several weeks. One groomer puts the heaviest season at April to June in the Northern Hemisphere, and Sheltie owners on a breed forum describe about a month to six weeks before a dog starts to look normal again. No controlled study gives a fixed number.

How often do Shelties blow their coat?

One breeder reports that males generally blow coat once a year, while intact females generally do so every 6 to 9 months, after coming into season. Forum owners often describe one or two blows a year.

Should I brush every day while my Sheltie is blowing coat?

A short daily session on one or two body zones is easier on the dog and on you than one long weekend session. Finish each section with a comb that reaches the skin.

Can I shave a Sheltie to stop the shedding?

Grooming professionals quoted by the AKC generally advise against shaving double-coated dogs, because the two layers grow back at different rates and the coat can regrow unevenly.

What brush is best during a coat blow?

A long-tined soft slicker for line brushing, a steel comb to check that you reached the skin, and an undercoat rake used with a light hand.

Does a bath help release the undercoat?

Many groomers wash, condition and blow-dry before the main brush-out. Brush out tangles first, rinse thoroughly and dry the coat completely.

Why does my Sheltie look thin after blowing coat?

The undercoat is what pushes the outer coat out. Once it is gone the dog looks narrower until new undercoat grows in.

The short version

A Sheltie blowing coat is a multi-week job. Line brush a zone a day, check with a comb, use one well-timed bath, skip the clippers, and let your tuft log tell you when the peak has passed. Save the dates: next season you will know when to start.
